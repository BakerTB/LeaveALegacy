What is Backtesting?
→Testing a predictive model on historical data — running the trading strategy's signals through past market data as if it were live, then measuring results.
→Helps estimate potential profitability, risk exposure, and reliability of the strategy before committing real capital.
→Test period: July 1, 2024 → July 1, 2025 — a full year of live market conditions across all volatility environments.
→Dataset: 122,148 total lines/trades analyzed using Alpaca Markets historical data.
Strategy Recap

Three independent indicators must align simultaneously before a trade is executed. The backtesting validated whether this confluence requirement creates a genuine edge.

→Fair Value Gaps (FVG): Identifies price inefficiency zones where the market moved too fast, likely to return and fill.
→Relative Strength Index (RSI): Momentum signal filtering entries to overbought/oversold extremes only.
→Support & Resistance Bands: Entries near key prior session levels where price historically reacts.
Moneyness Analysis

A key part of the backtesting was determining which options moneyness — ITM, ATM, or OTM — produced the best risk-adjusted returns for this strategy.

In the Money
ITM
Option has intrinsic value. Call: stock price > strike. Put: stock price < strike. Exercising would generate immediate profit.
Higher premium, lower % return
At the Money
ATM
Stock price = strike price. No intrinsic value, but the highest time value (extrinsic value) of any moneyness.
Highest time decay risk
Out of the Money
OTM
Option has no intrinsic value. Call: stock price < strike. Put: stock price > strike. Only time/extrinsic value remains.
Cheapest — highest leverage
Take Profit & Stop Loss

Exit management is as important as entry strategy. The backtesting included optimization of both take-profit targets and stop-loss levels to find the configuration that maximized the profit/loss ratio.

Take Profit (TP)
A predefined price level at which the position closes automatically to lock in gains. Backtesting compared multiple TP multiples (1.5×, 2×, 3× risk) against each other.
Stop Loss (SL)
A predefined level that closes the position if the trade moves against you, capping maximum loss per trade. Essential for long-term survival in options trading.
