📐The Formula
Black-Scholes Pricing Formula — European Call Option
C = S·N(d₁) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d₂)

d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T] / (σ·√T)
d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T

N(·) = cumulative standard normal distribution. The formula assumes log-normal price distribution, constant volatility, no dividends, and frictionless markets.

🔢The Five Inputs
S
Underlying Price
Current market price of the stock. Higher stock price → higher call value.
K
Strike Price
The price at which the option can be exercised. Further OTM → cheaper premium.
T
Time to Expiry
Measured in years. More time → more optionality → higher premium (theta decay).
r
Risk-Free Rate
Typically the 10-year treasury yield. Higher rates increase call values slightly.
σ
Implied Volatility
The most critical input — measures expected price uncertainty. Higher vol → more expensive options (vega).
💡What This Taught Me
→The Greeks come alive: Visualizing how option price moves as S, T, or σ changes makes delta, theta, and vega concrete — not just names on a formula sheet.
→Model limitations matter: Black-Scholes assumes constant volatility — but in reality, IV changes. The "volatility smile" is the market correcting for this assumption.
→Pricing ≠ value: A model price tells you what an option "should" cost given assumptions. The gap between model price and market price is where opportunity lives.
→This knowledge directly improved the Options Trading Bot — understanding how IV affects pricing helped tune the entry parameters more precisely.
