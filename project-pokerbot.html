Layer 1 — Stat Tracking Observe

Before exploiting anyone, the bot needs to know who it's up against. Layer 1 builds a behavioral profile for every opponent by watching their actions across every hand and updating a running stat sheet.

Fold-to-3bet Rate
How often does this player fold when re-raised preflop? A rate above ~55% marks them as a target for frequent 3-bets, regardless of hole cards.
C-bet Frequency
When we check the flop, do they bet? High c-betters get check-raised or trapped. Low c-betters get attacked when we have any piece of the board.
River Call Rate
How often do they call a bet on the river? A rate ≥ 60% marks a "calling station" — the ideal target for massive overbets with strong hands.
Stat lookup logic
# Returns default estimate if we have < 3 data points
fold_to_3bet(pid) = fold_3bet / face_3bet if face_3bet >= 3 else 0.45
cbet_freq(pid) = cbet_took / cbet_opp if cbet_opp >= 3 else 0.60
river_call_rate(pid)= call_river / face_river if face_river >= 3 else 0.50

Stats are inferred from state snapshots — no hand-history files needed. The bot reconciles pre/post-street contributions to detect folds, calls, and raises by other players even when they aren't the one acting.

Layer 2 — Exploit Leaks Attack

Once the stats are reliable, Layer 2 turns tendencies into targets. Each opponent's biggest weakness becomes the bot's primary line of attack against them.

→ Attack folders preflop: If fold-to-3bet ≥ 55%, the bot 3-bets that player's opens with a much wider range than normal — turning marginal holdings into profitable bluffs purely because the opponent folds too often.
→ Overbet rivers vs calling stations: Against opponents with call rate ≥ 60%, bets on the river are sized 1.4–2× pot instead of the standard 0.75×. Calling stations can't fold correctly — so when the bot has a strong hand, it charges maximum.
→ Relentless c-betting vs passive opponents: If the bot raised preflop and the opponent rarely bets when checked to (low c-bet frequency), the bot fires a continuation bet on the flop regardless of whether it connected — taking down pots with air.

Layer 3 — Model Poisoning Deceive

A bot that exploits predictably becomes predictable itself. Layer 3 makes the bot's behavior look random to opponents — but it's actually deterministic randomness, seeded per hand and street so decisions stay consistent across retries without being repeatable across hands.

→ MD5-keyed PRNG: Every decision point gets a stable float in [0,1) derived from MD5(hand_id + ":" + street + ":" + seat). Same hand, same street, same result — but completely different the next hand.
→ Flop check-traps (~30%): When holding a very strong hand on the flop (flush or better), the bot checks instead of betting 30% of the time. The opponent bets into the trap on the turn, then gets raised or bombed — breaking the assumption that the bot always bets strong hands immediately.
→ Delayed turn bombs: When the bot checked the flop (trap or no-bet), it comes out firing 0.9–1.2× pot on the turn even with moderate holdings. Opponents can't distinguish the trap from a late-street bluff.
→ Randomized open sizing: Preflop open raises vary between 0.8–1.2× pot based on the PRNG. Strong hands don't always open bigger — eliminating the standard "bet-size tells" that most bots leak.

Custom Hand Evaluator

Rather than importing a poker library, the bot uses a hand evaluator written from scratch. It ranks any 5-or-more-card combination and returns a comparable tuple used for all decision logic.

→Handles all nine categories from high card (0) through straight flush (8). Returns a ranked tuple so hands can be directly compared with Python's > operator.
→Correctly handles Ace-low straights (A-2-3-4-5 "wheel"), flush detection across any suit, and full-house vs. trips disambiguation via Counter on rank frequencies.

Preflop Tier System

Rather than memorizing a lookup table of every preflop hand, the bot classifies hole cards into five tiers based on rank, pairing, and suitedness. This drives open sizing, 3-bet thresholds, and all-in thresholds.

TierLabelExample HandsDefault Action
0Muck72o, 83o, 94oFold (check if free)
1MarginalSuited connectors (65s), low pairsCall small opens, fold big ones
2DecentKJo, AT, medium pairs (77–88)Open for 0.75× pot, call medium

Street-by-Street Decision Logic
→ Preflop: Classify hand tier → decide to fold, call, open, or 3-bet. If facing a raise with a tier-4 hand, always 3-bet. If facing a raise with a tier-3 hand, 3-bet only if the target has a high fold-to-3bet rate (Layer 2).
→ Flop: Check for trap opportunity (Layer 3). If betting, size at 50–70% pot for made hands. Bluff c-bet ~35% of the time as the preflop aggressor. Check-raise with strong hands ~40% of the time when facing a flop bet.
→ Turn: If trapped on the flop, fire 0.9–1.2× pot delayed bomb (Layer 3). Otherwise continue value-betting strong hands and check/fold weaker ones.
→ River: Max value mode — size based on opponent call rate (Layer 2). Bluff ~25% of the time as the preflop aggressor with one pair on the river. Check-raise strong hands ~35% when facing a river bet.

Key Result
A fully autonomous poker bot that connects to a live server, identifies opponent weaknesses across every hand, exploits them with targeted bet sizing, and actively masks its own patterns with deterministic randomness. The three-layer architecture separates observation, exploitation, and deception into clean independent systems — meaning each layer can be tuned or replaced without breaking the others.
